The full run

Every call, check and fix behind the dispute SOP check.

When a US customer calls about a charge they didn't make, the agent's answer is governed by Regulation E. This pack turns the rules into caller scenarios and pass/fail checks, then runs them on a fictional bank's first-draft dispute procedure, fixes what fails, and reruns until it passes.

Why this exists

Disputes are a named use case

Lorikeet's Forward Deployed PM posting opens with a stolen card, a disputed payment or a patient asking about their prescription. For US fintechs, the disputed payment comes with federal rules attached.

The mistakes are documented

CFPB's own FAQ calls out requiring police reports, sending customers to the merchant first, and counting 60 days from the transaction date, and notes examiners have cited these kinds of errors. A first-draft agent procedure can copy all of them.

Solve it once

Every US fintech deployment on disputes needs the same checks. The posting asks for solve it once rather than five times. This is that, for Reg E.

    How a team would use it

    1. Load the 17 scenarios into the deployment's simulation suite as test customers, with the caller line as the opening message.
    2. Turn each check into a QA rubric item or a reply guardrail. They are written in plain language on purpose.
    3. Run the customer's draft procedure, fix what fails, rerun, and only go live when every critical check passes.
    4. Rerun the pack whenever the dispute workflow or the underlying model changes.
